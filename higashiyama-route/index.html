A Friendly Recommendation from Amazing Kyoto

Higashiyama, As It Was

Gion is Kyoto's best-preserved historic quarter — the geisha district, and the streets where old Kyoto's wooden townscape survives almost untouched.

Route at a glance

🕒 About 2–3 hours, all on foot ⛰️ Only real incline: the lanes up to Kiyomizu-dera (Ninenzaka/Sannenzaka) — everything else is flat
1
First stop · 5 min from Sanjo Keihan Station

Tatsumi Bridge, Shirakawa

Gion's most photographed corner — and a filming location for Memoirs of a Geisha.

2
Gion lanes

Hanamikoji-dori

Real geisha district — come back after dark and you might spot a maiko slipping between engagements.

On the way: Kennin-ji, Kyoto's oldest Zen temple (1202), right at the street's south end.
Open in Google Maps →
3
The postcard shot

Yasaka Pagoda (Hōkan-ji)

One of Kyoto's defining landmarks — a five-story pagoda dating back to 1440. The surrounding lanes (Ninenzaka & Sannenzaka) are lined with old machiya townhouses turned into craft shops — one of the most atmospheric stretches in the city.

Conan fans: this exact stretch is where Ran, Sonoko & Kazuha walked in The Crossroad in the Ancient Capital.
Open in Google Maps →
4
Turn-back point · optional

Kiyomizu-dera

UNESCO World Heritage Site. Famous for the wooden stage built without a single nail — the view over Kyoto is the reward for the climb.

Conan fans: also a filming location in the same movie, right before Yasaka Pagoda.
Open in Google Maps →
5
Free · on the way back

Nene-no-Michi

A quiet stone-paved lane skirting Kōdai-ji's bamboo grove — no entry fee, no temple visit needed. This is the calm, unhurried stretch of the walk: just the bamboo, a few artisan shops, and a break from the crowds before Yasaka Shrine.

6
Free · 24hrs

Yasaka Shrine

The spiritual heart of Gion — free entry, open 24 hours, glowing after dark.

Open in Google Maps →
7
End here · dinner

Pontocho

Kyoto's most atmospheric dining alley, best after dusk — paper lanterns, and sometimes live shamisen drifting out of the teahouses.

Picking a restaurant: a queue of local faces beats a multilingual menu board. Conveyor sushi is always a safe, no-surprises option.
